How usage data is calculated

Where the numbers on Stats and Extra come from, and what each term means. All of it is computed on your phone.

On this page
  1. Where the data comes from
  2. Screen time
  3. Unlocks, wake-ups and sessions
  4. Distraction and apps
  5. Focus and blocked attempts
  6. Patterns, records and nudges

These are the definitions behind the numbers on the Stats and Extra screens. Everything is worked out on the phone; nothing is uploaded.

Where the data comes from

QuestionAnswer
What is it based on?Android’s own usage records, which FocusFlow can read once you allow Usage Access. Everything is worked out on this phone. Nothing is uploaded.
How far back does it go?The first time, FocusFlow reads up to 14 days of history, as much as Android still keeps. After that each finished day is saved as a small daily summary, kept for up to 365 days.
Why is today different?Today is read live and keeps changing until midnight. Days follow your phone’s own clock and time zone.
Complete vs partial daysA complete day has all of its records. A partial day is one FocusFlow could not record completely. It is shown but left out of averages and insights so it cannot pull them down.

Screen time

TermMeaning
Screen timeThe time the screen was on, from Android’s screen on and off events, split across the hours it covered.
Observed Device TimeThe time apps were open in the foreground. FocusFlow’s own home screen is left out so it does not count as phone use.
Daily averagesMade from complete days only, so one half-recorded day cannot skew them.

Unlocks, wake-ups and sessions

TermMeaning
UnlockThe phone being unlocked. A wake-up is the screen turning on at all, so it also counts a glance at a notification.
No screen lock?Android then reports no unlocks, so wake-ups are shown instead and sessions run from screen-on to screen-off.
Session / quick checkA session runs from unlock to screen-off. A quick check is a session shorter than 60 seconds. Session lengths are grouped as Under 30s, 30s–1m, 1–5m, 5–20m and 20m+.
First app after unlockingThe first app opened within 15 seconds of an unlock.
Gap between unlocksMeasured from one unlock to the next. Gaps longer than 3 hours (overnight, say) are not counted.
Parts of the dayMorning is 5 AM to noon, afternoon noon to 5 PM, evening 5 to 10 PM, and late night 10 PM to 5 AM. The busiest hour is the clock hour with the most screen time or unlocks.

Distraction and apps

TermMeaning
Distracting appsApps in the Social media, Entertainment and Games categories, plus any app you tried to open while it was blocked. Categories come from a built-in list; apps it does not know are shown as Other.
Reflex appAn app you open at least 8 times on the days you use it, mostly for short visits, across at least 3 days.
Rising appAn app whose daily average is at least 1.5× the previous period and 15 minutes more, from a starting point of at least 5 minutes.

Focus and blocked attempts

TermMeaning
HeldA focus session you finished without any overrides.
Blocked attemptA time you tried to open an app that FocusFlow was blocking. Attempts per focus hour is attempts divided by hours of focus.

Patterns, records and nudges

QuestionAnswer
How patterns are foundFrom up to 90 days of complete days, compared with earlier weeks. A change is only reported when it is big enough to matter and there are enough days behind it; each pattern says how sure it is.
Are these advice?No. They are observations about your own habits, not advice or a diagnosis.
NudgesOptional notifications, off by default. FocusFlow sends one 15 minutes before the hour a distracting app is used most, or before bedtime when late-night use is rising.
How many?At most 3 a day, never closer than 30 minutes apart. They are planned again every day from your latest data, so they stop when the pattern does.
Turn them offAny time, from the nudge switch on the Habits tab in Extra.
